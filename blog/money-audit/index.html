Money audit

The bank said 350,000. It was 70,000.

17 Aug 2026 · one day · open banking + SQLite + agents that argue with each other

Cancelling my father's junk app subscriptions took an evening. It also left an obvious question: if 16,000 a year could sit there unnoticed, what else was? So I connected both my parents' banks and cards through open banking, put a year of transactions in a local database, and asked. The first answer was terrifying and wrong by a factor of ten. This is what it took to find that out before I said it out loud.

Statements are the wrong interface

Nine institutions. Eighty-three accounts once you count every card, loan, savings plan and pension. Two people. A year of history is roughly 2,400 transactions, arriving as one PDF per month per account.

No human reads that. Neither does a language model, usefully - paging PDFs into context is expensive and lossy, and the interesting patterns are cross-account by nature. A charge is not suspicious on its own statement. It is suspicious because it repeats every week, on a card that also carries 17% interest, while cash sits idle in a different bank.

So the shape of this is boring and that is the point: get the rows, put them in SQLite, ask in SQL.

python3 openbank.py pull              # aggregator API -> local snapshot.db
python3 openbank.py owners            # every owner string the banks returned
python3 openbank.py sql "SELECT ..."  # investigate

What it costs, honestly

Israeli open banking (חוק שירות מידע פיננסי, live since 2023) opens bank APIs only to a licensed information-service provider. There is no consumer tier. So for anyone else's accounts you are not paying for a wrapper, you are renting a licence - which is why the instinct that an aggregator is an unnecessary middleman is right for your own money and wrong for your parents'. In the EU and UK the PSD2 equivalent works the same way.

The finding that was wrong

The first pass came back with a number I did not want to carry into a room with two pensioners in it: 26 loan accounts, about ILS 350,000 outstanding.

That would have changed their lives. It was also nonsense, and the reason is a data-format detail nobody outside a card issuer would ever guess.

An Israeli card issuer reports a revolving credit product as one row per monthly cycle. Same account number, a different contract start date each month. Thirteen months on file reads as thirteen separate loans.

Summing them multiplied the debt by the number of cycles on record. The correct read is the latest snapshot per distinct account number:

-- WRONG
SELECT SUM(balance) FROM accounts WHERE account_type='loan';

-- RIGHT
WITH latest AS (
  SELECT account_number, MAX(reference_date) rd FROM accounts
  WHERE account_type='loan' GROUP BY 1)
SELECT a.person, a.account_number, a.balance FROM accounts a
JOIN latest l ON l.account_number = a.account_number
             AND l.rd = a.reference_date;

Real figure: ILS 70,307 across both of them. Serious, fixable, not a catastrophe.

The second error was quieter and would have survived a casual review. A verification agent disagreed with my total and, on inspection, it was the one that was wrong: it had filtered by the owner's name in Hebrew, and two of her cards carry her name in Latin characters. Whole accounts vanished from the total because of a spelling. That is why the rule in the skill is: always filter on the resolved person, never on the raw owner string the bank gives you.

The actual lesson: not "AI gets things wrong". It is that I could afford to run three independent skeptics against my own conclusion, for cents, before it reached a frightened 83-year-old. Most people never get a second opinion on their money. Here the second, third and fourth cost less than the coffee I drank reading them.

Then it also cleared two people I had accused

The same pass ran in the other direction. Two merchants I had confidently flagged as scams came back clean: one is his real e-cigarette supplier, with genuine order confirmations in his mailbox; the other is a legitimate wellness service for seniors that he may well have signed up for on purpose. Both were one dispute letter away from being sent.

The transaction data alone cannot tell a scam from a supplier. The mailbox settles it. Check the ground truth before you accuse anyone of anything - especially when the person you are about to embarrass in front of their own family is your parent.

Total recoverable, without cutting a single thing they enjoy: ILS 3,000-4,500 a month. That is a trip abroad every year, funded entirely by things that were taking money for nothing.

Findings do not change anything. Actions do.

The part I would have skipped a year ago: after the report, agents drove their own browsers and mailboxes, with them present and approving each item, to actually do it. Every junk subscription cancelled with the cancellation date recorded, 40+ refund requests filed and confirmed in writing, cancellation emails sent to the services with no cancel button, and one live Hebrew page with a tick-box list of what is left - the items that need their password, their phone, or a visit to the bank.

A PDF of findings handed to two people in their eighties changes nothing at all. Sorted by monthly recovery, in their language, with the things I can do already done, is a different object.

The rule I would not bend: nothing gets cancelled without an item-by-item yes. "Clean it all up" is not consent for a specific cancellation, and being right about the money does not make you entitled to make decisions about someone else's.

If you want to do this for your own family

  1. They link their accounts themselves, in the aggregator's UI. There is no API for that, and there should not be.
  2. Pull it into SQLite. Query it. Never state a number you did not get from a query.
  3. Before you tell them anything, make something independent re-derive it. Ask specifically: could one thing be counted twice, and could one thing be missing because of a name? Those two questions caught both of my errors.
  4. Check the mailbox before calling any merchant a fraud.
  5. Present decisions, not a report, sorted by what it saves a month.

The skill

The whole pipeline is packaged as an installable agent skill - the aggregator pull, the per-person ownership resolver, the query playbook, both traps written down so they cost you nothing, and the verification protocol.
